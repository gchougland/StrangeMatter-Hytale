# Laboratory equipment

Field scanning and Echo Vacuum extraction use native two-second charging interactions. The server verifies the same anomaly, uninterrupted aim, line of sight, held equipment and range at completion. Only the original six natural anomaly types grant observations; each awards the original default 10 points once per researcher. Released or artificial anomalies cannot replenish this research economy.

Empty capsules are filled by the vacuum, matching the Minecraft source. A singleton capsule replaces its own inventory slot, including in a full inventory; stacked empties require space for the uniquely tagged filled capsule. Failed delivery rolls the capture back. Filled capsules are thrown as moving native models with a launch effect, gravity, swept block collision and a distinct impact effect. Exactly one capsule is consumed in survival, and the original anomaly identity is released on impact. The source consumes a filled capsule without returning an empty one. Tagged capsule flights now have a persistent write-ahead queue containing their complete native item payload, owner, world, trajectory and identity nonce. Required native inventory saves acknowledge survival consumption before movement begins. Creative throws change no inventory and therefore start from the durable flight phase without waiting on a needless player save. Input roots require a fresh click, so holding the button cannot repeatedly enqueue a unique capsule. Deferred actions resolve the current native inventory component and selected slot; an atomic expected-value replacement reserves exactly one full payload. A pending throw reports its actual save/flight/refund status instead of an ambiguous launch error. Restart recovery reconciles old copies in the owner's backpack, storage and hotbar before resuming. A flight reaching unloaded terrain or its time limit enters a durable refund queue; the intact capsule returns to its owner's inventory once space is available, and its required save completes before retirement. World unload preserves flights. See [capsule-recovery.md](capsule-recovery.md) for save-boundary details and remaining native transaction limits.

The Chrono Blister fires a moving temporal orb after a full one-second charge. Block or creature impact places the original radius-two ragged volume: a complete core within radius 1.7 and a 70% shell out to radius 2.5. These are pass-through `SM_Time_Dilation_Block` voxels, each with an independent 200–600 tick lifespan. The machine ledger stores age and lifespan, so fields expire correctly after a server restart and pause while their chunk is unloaded. Native effects reduce creature/player horizontal speed to 30%; nonliving velocity and vertical creature velocity are damped once per simulation tick. Overlapping voxels deduplicate affected entities. Hytale's global time modifier is not changed, and the Minecraft client-side use-animation timing hook is not reproduced.

Warp-gun inventory updates are checked before replacing the old aperture. Failed item updates remove the newly created aperture and keep the old one. Broken durable tools cannot activate, while clearing a warp pair remains possible. Creative shots do not spend durability. The original vacuum declares durability but does not subtract it on capture, and the original Graviton Hammer has unlimited durability; those source behaviors are preserved.

The hammer mines a 3×3 surface, or one block while crouching, and native one/two/three-second charges extend its tunnel to 3/6/9 blocks. All six view directions produce nine distinct cells in an orthogonal plane; the downward case no longer repeats a three-block diagonal. Each swing uses the loaded native Thorium pickaxe tool profile and Hytale's normal block-health damage, gathering, drops and cancellable protection events. Primary stone mining requires successive swings. Charged stages apply 2/3/4 times the native block-damage power; a first-stage charged impact can fracture ordinary stone in one hit. The requested charged combat slam is an explicit playtest addition beyond the original mining-only charge: a four-block forward arc deals 20/30/40 native Physical damage after one/two/three seconds, using an attributed native damage event with armor, PvP and invulnerability filters. It checks line of sight and never strikes behind the wielder. Primary entity combat behavior is unchanged. Native primary input and the server both enforce the normal quarter-second pickaxe swing cadence. Protected/unbreakable cells and laboratory machines are skipped; native gathering alone decides which damaged blocks actually break.

The stasis projector independently captures one NPC and one dropped item, excludes players and mount entities, and displays them at the source's bobbing capture height above the projector. A finite native effect disables movement and attacks and grants temporary invulnerability. Item pickup delay is refreshed while captured. Removing or disabling the projector stops holding immediately; effects and pickup delay expire within 0.7 seconds. No permanent frozen, AI-disabled or invulnerable component is left behind. Levitation includes dropped items through Hytale's separate item spatial index.

The hoverboard's owned `NPCMountComponent` is attached to its holder before native NPC spawn. This is essential: Hytale's `NPCMountSystems.OnAdd` is an entity-add system, so adding the component afterward never sends a mount packet. The board enters the native mount lifecycle, inherits the complete native horse `Mount` movement asset, emits engine exhaust, and folds away when dismounted. The previous partial movement asset left omitted speed/steering fields at zero; its valid mount packet could therefore seat a player while preventing movement. The inherited configuration restores the native speed limit, direction weights, acceleration, jumping and collision settings. The mounted role follows native `Empty_Role` behavior with a Walk controller and empty instructions, and the service no longer zeros velocity every tick. Entity model scale and rider anchor are doubled; the board spawns with the bottom of its scaled collider at the rider's feet rather than embedded in terrain. The server reasserts the mount packet once after the entity tracker has sent the new board to its rider. The board is transient; deployment consumes the exact inventory item after required native save acknowledgements. Dismounting removes the entity and returns its full payload through a persistent recovery receipt, including when inventory space becomes available later. Native NPC simulation is suspended while rider movement remains active, eliminating competing server gravity on downhill steps. See [mobility-port.md](mobility-port.md) for identity, saves and recovery.

Echoform retains the player's physical body and changes only appearance. Hold primary on a living target for one second to imprint; Secondary or Use explicitly restores the original form. Crouch-primary is also retained as a convenience restore. Restoring is allowed even when the imprinter is depleted and does not require a valid aim target; new disguises still respect durability. The native HUD shows the current disguise and scan progress.

Gadgets use a keyed native HUD that coexists with other mods. Scanner/vacuum beams, lock and completion feedback, capsule launch/impact, chrono muzzle/trail/impact, hammer pulses, imprint/restore effects and hoverboard engagement/exhaust have dedicated finite particles and positional sounds. The resonator reports frequency, direction, elevation and distance without chat spam. Warp-gun endpoints retain their cyan/purple channel metadata, and the HUD shows whether each aperture is projected. Levitation direction arrows span the usable shaft with range rings at both ends; a toggle sound accompanies a direction change. The flattened stasis pad anchors its beam at y+0.2 and shows the bobbing held specimen above it. The removed anomalous-grass hoe interaction and prompt are no longer part of equipment dispatch.

Research Notes show the experiment's title and description. Using a note while aiming at a Research Machine selects that exact note there; using it elsewhere shows an instrument hint rather than opening the Research Tablet archive.
